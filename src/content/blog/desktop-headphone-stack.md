## One source, different perspectives

My desktop audio system is built around a FiiO K13 R2R and a small rotation of headphones. The appeal is not that one pairing is universally correct. It is that the same source makes the differences between headphones easy to understand.

The K13 keeps the chain compact: digital input, conversion, volume control, and enough amplification for the headphones I use most. That reduces the temptation to treat every change in sound as a reason to add another box.

## Audeze LCD-X: physical and grounded

The LCD-X is the most physically assertive headphone in the rotation. It gives low frequencies weight and texture without making the presentation feel soft. Dense recordings remain composed, and electronic music has the scale I want from a planar driver.

It is also heavy. Comfort and listening time are part of the system, not footnotes. The LCD-X is what I choose when I want impact and focus, not necessarily what I leave on for an entire day.

## HIFIMAN Edition XS: width and openness

The Edition XS provides the clearest contrast. Its presentation feels wider and more open, which makes layered recordings and ambient music easier to explore. The bass reaches deep, but the impression is more spacious than physical.

Fit is unusually personal with this headphone. On the right head, its large frame disappears; on the wrong one, the scale becomes a distraction. That is a useful reminder that frequency response is only one part of whether a headphone works.

## Sennheiser HD 660S2: intimate and easy to return to

The HD 660S2 brings the image closer. Vocals and midrange detail feel more immediate, and the lighter frame makes it an easy choice for longer sessions. It does not create the Edition XS’s stage or the LCD-X’s physical low end, but its balance makes it dependable.

That difference is why all three remain useful. They are not three attempts to solve the same problem:

- LCD-X for density, impact, and low-end texture;
- Edition XS for stage, air, and planar scale;
- HD 660S2 for intimacy, comfort, and an easy everyday presentation.

## What the R2R stage contributes

I am careful about assigning dramatic language to DACs. The transducer and fit dominate the experience. What I value about the K13 R2R is the complete package: a smooth desktop workflow, enough power, straightforward switching, and a presentation that does not push me away from longer listening.

The practical qualities matter every day. A device can measure well or look impressive and still be annoying to operate. The K13 stays because it reduces friction.

> The best component in the stack is often the one that makes you stop thinking about the stack.

## How I evaluate the system

My preferences lean warm, with deep sub-bass, but I listen for more than tuning. Stage and image placement matter for complex recordings. Detail matters when it reveals texture rather than sharpness. Comfort decides whether any of those qualities survive a long session.

Chain synergy is partly about sound and partly about use. The same headphone can feel more valuable when it is connected to a source that is always ready and easy to control.

## A system, not a leaderboard

I do not need one winner. The rotation works because each headphone changes the perspective while the rest of the chain stays stable. That makes listening more intentional and keeps the gear from becoming a collection without roles.
